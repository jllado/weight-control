# Weekly review design

The approved review separates Overview, Measurements and Reflection & actions while retaining the app's compact controls, existing status vocabulary and saved evidence.

Overview leads with daily routine completion, the week's distribution and three lowest-completion routines. Weekly averages and dated weekend readings stay separate. Compact workout comparisons and seven nutrient rows remain directly visible, followed by the saved reflection takeaway. Detailed comparisons link to the relevant measurement group.

Measurements groups body and pressure, sleep and mood, training and back pain, nutrition, routines and other metrics. Tables show one metric per row with units, dated comparison columns and signed changes separate from absolute status. Narrow screens stack each metric's dated values. Daily routines are searchable and weekly routines remain separate.

Reflection & actions shows three saved next-week priorities, with further recommendations and topic evidence disclosed on demand. Action identity, deduplication, activation, existing wording, records, warning progress and goal context remain intact.

Use saved app classifications where available; do not introduce a generic “On track” label or invent historical classifications. Missing historical rules or dated plan evidence remain neutral. A recorded change is not itself a health assessment.

Vitamin D uses a neutral reference by default; an explicit personal intake goal enables normal comparison. Omega-3 retains the measured total but checks EPA+DHA and ALA independently: both minima established is met, either supported shortfall is below, otherwise unknown. Main views show neither subtype chemistry nor specific food prescriptions. Reference detail remains optional. Apply these meanings to daily values, 30-day trends, charts, saved weekly reports, future emails and Coach; preserve food provenance and historical snapshots.

New schema-10 reports capture dated workout targets, classification rules and precise nutrient assessment values. Sleep classifications reuse the existing four-criterion score (time in bed, total sleep, efficiency and midpoint consistency) from sufficient complete dated records; duration alone supports only a sleep-goal comparison. Existing reports never read current profile settings. The manually checked weekly routine “Have fish for dinner once a week” is a separate user commitment, with no automatic nutrition inference, reminders or historical completions.

Acceptance covers keyboard navigation, responsive layouts, sparse and legacy evidence, reference versus personal goals, independent omega-3 coverage, partial totals, dated plan comparisons, immutable history and action activation. Release requires independent candidate QA, the complete artifact gate, application identity/readiness verification and private Coach publication/readback.
